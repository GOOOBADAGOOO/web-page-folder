how tortillas are made for nachos

Field corn is the starting point for many of our favorite snacks, including nachos. First, the corn is boiled in water mixed with calcium hydroxide for about 30 to 90 minutes, then left to soak for 8 to 16 hours. This special process, called nixtamalization, softens the kernels, loosens the outer hulls, and even improves the corn’s nutritional value by making certain vitamins easier for our bodies to absorb.

After soaking, the corn is rinsed carefully to remove the loosened skins and any remaining calcium solution. At this stage, the kernels are softer and cleaner. They are then ground, traditionally using heavy millstones, into a smooth, moist dough called masa. Masa is the base ingredient for many corn foods in Mexican cuisine.

To make tortilla chips for nachos, the fresh masa is first shaped into flat, round tortillas. These tortillas are usually pressed into thin discs and cooked briefly on a very hot, dry griddle at about 450°F to 500°F. They are heated for around 30 seconds on each side, just until they puff slightly and show light brown or dark spots. This step gives the tortillas their structure and basic flavor.

Once the tortillas are cooked, they are cut into triangle-shaped pieces to form the familiar chip shape. These pieces are then fried in hot oil until they become crisp and golden. After frying, the chips are drained and lightly salted. At this point
